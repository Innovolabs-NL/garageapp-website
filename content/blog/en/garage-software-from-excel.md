Many shops still run on Excel lists, Access databases, or a mix of email and paper. It works — until job volume grows, a tech is out sick, or a customer asks: “when is my car ready?”

## The problem isn’t “no software”

It’s **fragmentation**. Intake in one file, inspection in Word, hours on a slip, invoices in another tool. Every manual handoff is an error and a delay. Customers have no status view. Technicians chase the latest work plan. The office retypes the same plate and customer data at invoicing time.

## What “one system” means

Real garage software connects the job lifecycle end-to-end:

1. Customer and vehicle (plate, history)
2. Inspection / intake on the floor
3. Work plan and customer approval (print PDF in-shop or secure link with signature + timestamp)
4. Work order, scheduling, and capacity
5. Time tracking (timer or manual) plus parts and consumables
6. Invoice (proforma or final PDF) and customer visibility in the portal

Without that chain, “going digital” is cosmetic: you have tools, but you still retype.

## Checklist before you switch

1. **Where do you retype today?** List handoffs (inspection → quote → work order → hours → invoice).
2. **Who needs to see what?** Office, technician, and customer need different rights; one shared spreadsheet is not a portal.
3. **How does the customer approve?** In the shop (print) or remotely (link + signature)?
4. **Invoicing and payment:** PDF only, or online pay via Mollie/Tikkie?
5. **Trial:** walk your real flow, not a generic feature tour.
